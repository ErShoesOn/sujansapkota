## What is an Extension of Time?

An Extension of Time (EOT) is a formal extension of the contract completion period. It is requested when progress on the work is affected by events that are recognised under the contract and that are beyond the contractor's control. The decision on whether to grant an extension belongs to the procuring entity under the contract terms.

This article provides general education. Your contract documents, their conditions, and the applicable official rules determine what applies to your specific project.

## When might a contractor consider requesting EOT?

Situations commonly considered include:

- Delays in handing over the site or access needed to start work.
- Changes instructed after the contract was signed that affect the programme.
- Unusually adverse site or weather conditions recognised by the contract.
- Delays caused by events expressly identified in the contract documents.
- Other circumstances that the contract itself allows as grounds for extension.

The key question is always: **what does the contract say?** A general expectation of delay is not the same as a contractual ground.

## What records support an EOT request?

Good EOT requests are built on records created while the work is happening, not reconstructed later.

| Record | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Contract programme and revisions | Shows the planned completion date and critical path |
| Site instructions and correspondence | Links the cause of delay to an instructed event |
| Daily reports and site logs | Establishes what actually happened and when |
| Communications about access or approvals | Shows where delays originated |
| Photos and progress records | Supports the factual timeline |
| Notice of delay and follow-ups | Shows the request was made in a timely manner |

## What does a practical EOT process look like?

1. **Notice:** Send the notice required by the contract as soon as the delay becomes apparent.
2. **Record:** Keep a factual timeline of events, dates and effects on work.
3. **Relate to the contract:** Identify the specific contractual ground you are relying on.
4. **Explain the effect:** Show how the event affected the completion date, not just the work generally.
5. **Submit the request:** Follow the submission format and timelines stated in the contract.
6. **Respond to queries:** Provide clarified records if the procuring entity asks for them.

## How is this different from a variation order?

A **variation order** changes the scope, specification or quantities of the work. An **Extension of Time** changes the time available for completion. A variation may lead to an EOT if it affects the programme, but they are separate contractual actions with separate records and decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is EOT automatic when it rains?

No. Whether weather conditions support an EOT depends on the contract terms, the actual conditions and the records. General seasonal conditions are usually treated differently from abnormal events.

### Can I request EOT after the completion date has passed?

The contract usually sets notice and submission requirements. Late requests can be difficult to support. Send the required notice as soon as a delay becomes apparent and follow the contract procedure.

### Does EOT mean extra payment?

Not necessarily. EOT deals with time. Cost implications are handled separately under the contract terms.

### Can a consultant guarantee that EOT will be approved?

No. The decision rests with the procuring entity. Professional support can help you document the request clearly and on time.
